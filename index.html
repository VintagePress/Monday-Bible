This is the single source of truth for everything a CSM needs when talking to a client about monday AI - how it works, how it's controlled, how to build trust, and how to tell if the timing is right.

Part 1

Information Gaps

How monday AI works. These are the facts - the goal is that every CSM gives the same answer, every time, with a link to the source.

What monday AI includes

Owner: Product · Verified

monday AI is not one feature - it's a set of AI capabilities across the platform, all measured in the same AI credits:

  • AI blocks - AI steps inside boards and automations, like summarize, categorize, extract or write.
  • monday sidekick - an AI assistant that works with the user's boards and data.
  • monday agents - AI agents that take actions in the account, not just answer.
  • AI workflows - multi-step workflows that combine AI steps and human approvals.
  • AI Notetaker - records and summarizes meetings.
  • monday vibe - builds custom apps from a prompt.

Source: The pricing model for monday AI portfolio

How client data is used (and not used)

Owner: Security · Verified

Client data is not used to train AI models - not by monday.com, and not by the AI model providers.

  • The model providers work under Zero Data Retention. They don't store data processed through monday AI, and the agreements don't allow them to use it for anything other than processing the request.
  • When models run on monday-controlled cloud services (AWS Bedrock, Azure AI, Google Vertex), the model provider has no access to the data at all.
  • The client owns both the content they put into monday AI and the content it generates.
  • Data is encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3).

Two details legal teams usually find

It's better if the CSM mentions these before the client's legal team finds them:

  • 60-day access for improvement. monday.com may temporarily access interaction data (up to 60 days) to monitor and improve the service. It's never shared or used for training. An admin can opt out by emailing ai-support@monday.com or through their account representative (up to 5 business days).
  • Some models keep data for 30 days. A few models (for example Anthropic's Fable 5) may keep input and output for 30 days, only for harm prevention and never for training. These models are enabled in the admin settings.

Sources: AI FAQs · AI Trust Center · AI Terms

What happens to a request, step by step

Owner: Security · Verified
  1. A user (or an agent) sends a request inside monday.
  2. monday AI only uses data the user is allowed to see. If they can't access a board or column, AI won't use it.
  3. The request goes through monday's AI Gateway, which applies rate limits, token limits, moderation and security checks.
  4. An AI model processes the request through a managed cloud service, under zero retention.
  5. The result comes back into monday. The model provider doesn't keep the data or train on it.

Source: AI Trust Center - includes a data flow diagram that can be shared with security teams

Data residency

Owner: Legal · Verified

Data processed by monday AI follows the same data region settings as the client's monday account. There are separate sub-processor lists per region (for example EU and APAC).

Before promising anything, check which region the client's account is in, and send them the sub-processor list for that region.

Each plan has a minimum monthly amount of credits, and the client can choose a bigger bucket:

Estimating how many credits a client needs

Owner: Finance · Product gap

"It depends on complexity" is not an answer a CFO can work with. There's no calculator yet, but the published rates above are enough for a reasonable estimate:

  1. Start from the 1-2 workflows the client actually wants to start with, not "AI in general".
  2. Estimate the monthly volume of each, and multiply by the rates above.
  3. Pick the bucket that covers it, and set a usage limit (see Usage limits).
  4. After a month, replace the estimate with real numbers from the credit usage chart.

AI vs. the automations clients already have

Owner: Product · Draft

Automations follow fixed rules - "when X happens, do Y". They work best when the input is structured and the decision is always the same.

AI handles what rules can't - reading free text, summarizing, categorizing, writing, and deciding what to do with information that isn't structured.

AutomationAI
ExampleWhen status changes to Done, move the item to CompletedRead an incoming request, understand it, fill the right columns and draft a reply
InputStructured - status, date, numberUnstructured - text, emails, files, meetings
LogicFixed rulesUnderstands context

They're not either/or - AI can be a step inside an existing automation. The best places to start are where someone still has to read something and decide what to do with it.

Bring your own agent and MCP

Owner: Product · Verified

Bring your own agent means AI agents a client already uses outside monday can work inside monday like a team member - they can be assigned to items and mentioned in updates.

  • Connected agents use their own API token and only get the workspaces and boards the admin allows.
  • Their credit usage shows up in the account's usage dashboard.
  • Clients without external agents don't need this - monday's own agents work without it.

MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the standard way AI assistants like Claude or ChatGPT connect to other tools. monday has a hosted MCP server, so a client's existing AI assistant can read and work with their monday data, within the user's permissions.

Sources: Build on monday.com with AI · monday MCP overview

Part 2

Control

Every control the client has over monday AI. A client asking about control usually wants to move forward - on their own terms. Show them the controls exist, and help them set up a controlled start.

Turning AI on and off

Owner: Product · Verified

Account admins can turn monday AI on or off for the whole account. This is the "kill switch" compliance teams ask about.

To enable or disable AI on the entire account:

  1. Click the profile picture in the top right corner and choose Administration
  2. Open the AI Governance section and select Settings
  3. Find the Enable monday AI option and toggle it On or Off

When something goes wrong, the client usually doesn't need to turn everything off - on Enterprise they can limit a specific agent, feature or role instead (see Who can use what), or deactivate a single agent (see Agent directory).

Source: AI Permissions and Governance

Who can use what

Owner: Product · Verified

By default, when AI is on, all users in the account can use it. On the Enterprise plan, the AI permissions tab lets admins narrow this down:

  • Agents - for each agent type (user agents, monday agents, third-party agents, org agents and external AI connectors), choose which account roles can use it. Permissions can be set per agent, or inherited from the agent type.
  • AI features - each feature has its own setting, with the same role selection.
  • Workspaces - for sidekick and AI blocks, admins can also choose which workspaces the feature is on or off in.

Note: changing permissions won't stop or break AI blocks or agents that are already running.

On other plans, the main way to control who uses how much is user limit policies.

Source: AI Permissions and Governance

What AI can see

Owner: Security · Verified

monday AI respects the account's existing permissions. Users can only create, view or generate content from things they already have access to - if a user can't see a board or column, AI won't use it.

External agents connected to monday only get the workspaces and boards the admin allows.

Good practice for a first rollout: start on internal boards, and keep client-facing boards out of scope until the client is comfortable.

Sources: AI FAQs · AI Trust Center

Seeing where credits go

Owner: Finance · Verified

The Credit Usage tab in AI governance shows the credits used in the current billing cycle and the remaining balance.

  • A forecast chart shows cumulative usage so far, and projects it at the current rate, +10% and +20% - so the client can see early if they're on track to stay within their limit.
  • Breakdown by feature and by user, with filters to look at specific users.
  • Download of usage data for all users, for the client's finance team.

Note: the forecast chart only appears for accounts paying for AI credits, not for accounts on trial credits.

In the first months, suggest a short monthly look at this tab together with the client's admin.

Source: AI Permissions and Governance

Usage limits

Owner: Finance · Verified

The Usage limits tab lets admins cap AI credits at three levels:

  • Account - the total credits available for the billing cycle.
  • Per AI capability - a limit for a specific feature. Each limit is marked Hard or Soft, depending on how it's enforced. Features without a limit use credits as needed until the account limit.
  • Per user or department - a default limit for all users, plus separate policies for specific users or departments (for example power users who need more).

When the limit gets close

  • Admins get notified at 80% and 100% of usage.
  • Some AI capabilities keep running for a short time after the limit, so the team can top up before anything is paused.
  • Only AI usage pauses - boards, items and regular automations keep working.

Sources: AI Permissions and Governance · Pricing model

Agent directory - seeing every agent and what it did

Owner: Product · Verified

The Agent directory tab gives admins one place to see every AI agent running in the account, with its name, owner, sharing status, current status, what it has access to, creation date and the model it uses.

From each agent's menu, admins can:

  • View the agent's activity
  • Manage its tools (skills and access)
  • Transfer ownership
  • Deactivate or activate it

View connections shows who owns each agent, who it's shared with, and which boards it can access.

Source: AI Permissions and Governance

Human approval and logs

Owner: Product · Needs owner review
  • Approval steps - monday workflows can stop and wait for a named person to approve before continuing.
  • Test before live - a workflow can be run before it goes live, so the client sees exactly what it will do.
  • Logs - every workflow run is logged step by step, including agent activity.

monday also recommends that users review AI output - another reason to keep a human step at the start.

Owner to confirm: whether every agent type supports an approval step, whether activity logs show the agent's reasoning ("why") and not just the action, and the Legal-approved wording on accountability for AI output.

Part 3

Trust

How to show monday AI is worth it. Clients here may have been burned before or are tired of the hype - they need proof, not promises. The best proof is the client's own results from a small, measured start.

How reliable is it?

Owner: Product · Needs owner review

What monday does to make AI features reliable:

  • Every feature is tested before release, including by an internal red team that tries to break it and find edge cases.
  • Features are rolled out gradually to monitor stability before reaching everyone.
  • AI features are included in monday's external penetration tests.
  • Performance and behavior are monitored over time to catch problems early.

Clients don't want to be the test case. The way to handle it is to lower the risk, not argue with the concern - start with generally available features, on an internal workflow, with a human approval step.

Owner to provide: an up-to-date list of which AI features are generally available and which are in beta. CSMs shouldn't guess this.

Source: AI FAQs

Measuring value and ROI

Owner: CS Enablement · Draft

"It'll save you time" isn't an answer. The most believable ROI numbers are the client's own - and that needs a baseline measured before starting.

What to measure

  • Time per item (for example, minutes to handle a request)
  • Number of items per week
  • Error or rework rate

Simple formula

(time saved per item) x (items per month) x (hourly cost), compared to the credits used in the same period, from the Credit Usage tab.

Pilot: Side by Side

Owner: CS Leadership · Draft

For clients who need proof. Take one existing workflow, run AI in parallel to it, and compare the results while going over the agent activity together.

WorkflowRepetitive, high volume, internal and easy to measure

If the client had a failed AI pilot before, ask what went wrong. Usually it was too broad, had no clear success metric, or nobody owned it - design this one to avoid exactly that.

Pilot: Champion User

Owner: CS Leadership · Draft

For clients where the issue is people, not technology - senior people who set the tone are resistant, or the last new feature was never adopted.

  1. Choose one person the team listens to - even better if they're a skeptic.
  2. Give them a workflow that solves something that annoys them personally.
  3. After 2-4 weeks, they show the results to the team, in their own words.

monday AI and other AI tools

Owner: Product Marketing · Draft

General AI assistants (like ChatGPT Enterprise) are great for working with information the user brings to them. monday AI works where the work already lives:

  • Context - it knows the boards, items, owners and status, without copy-pasting.
  • Action - it fills columns, updates items and runs inside workflows, not just answers.
  • Governance - same permissions, admin controls and usage limits as the rest of the account.

It's not either/or. Through monday's MCP server (see Bring your own agent and MCP), tools like ChatGPT or Claude can connect to monday data, so the client keeps the tools they already pay for.

Part 4

Readiness

Sometimes it's just not the right time - budget, a migration, too many projects at once. This part isn't the focus of this version, but CSMs need the basics so they don't push at the wrong moment.

"Not now" or "no"?

Owner: CS Enablement · Draft

A readiness objection can be real timing, or a soft "no" because the client doesn't see the value yet:

Real timingSoft "no"
A specific reason with an end date - the migration ends in Q2, budget opens next quarterThe reason keeps changing
Agrees to a date to come back to itAvoids committing to any date
Still curious about what AI could do for themNot interested in hearing examples

If it looks like a soft "no", it's usually a trust issue - see Part 3.

When it's real timing

Owner: CS Enablement · Draft
  • Don't push - it hurts the relationship more than it helps adoption.
  • Set a concrete point to come back to it, like after the migration or at next quarter's budget.
  • If it's a small team with no one to own a rollout, the ask can be much smaller - one workflow, one person.

Concerns about AI replacing jobs

Owner: CS Leadership · Draft

This is a sensitive topic, and it deserves its own guidance in a future version. Until then:

  • Be honest, and don't make promises about the client's organization - it's not ours to promise.
  • Focus on the repetitive work people would be happy to hand off.
  • Bring in CS leadership if the client wants to go deeper.
